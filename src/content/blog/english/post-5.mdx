High availability and disaster recovery address related but different risks. High availability focuses on maintaining service during component or node failures, while disaster recovery addresses larger incidents that can affect an entire environment.

## Define recovery requirements

Start by defining two important business objectives:

- Recovery Time Objective (RTO): the acceptable time to restore service.
- Recovery Point Objective (RPO): the acceptable amount of data loss measured in time.

These objectives guide architecture, backup frequency, replication strategy and recovery procedures.

## Understand replication limitations

Replication can improve availability, but it is not a substitute for independent backups. Accidental deletions, logical corruption and unwanted changes may also be replicated.

Understand the failure scenarios covered by the chosen replication architecture.

## Maintain independent backups

Define backup frequency, retention, storage location, encryption and access controls. Consider keeping backup copies isolated from the primary database environment.

Monitor backup completion and investigate failures promptly.

## Test recovery procedures

A recovery plan should be executable, documented and tested. Validate restoration, database consistency, application connectivity and operational dependencies.

Record the time taken and any issues encountered during recovery exercises.

## Document failover responsibilities

Define who can initiate failover, how the recovery environment is validated and how applications are redirected. Include communication and decision-making responsibilities.

## Conclusion

A dependable resilience strategy combines appropriate availability architecture, independent backups, documented recovery procedures and regular testing. Recovery capability should be demonstrated, not merely assumed.
